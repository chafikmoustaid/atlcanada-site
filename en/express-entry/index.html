Express Entry: programs, points and 2026 changes

Express Entry manages permanent residence applications for three federal programs. Here are the basic requirements, how the ranking works and what has changed since November 3, 2026.

How it works

  1. You create an online profile and enter the pool of candidates.
  2. You receive a score under the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS), calculated from points.
  3. IRCC invites the top-ranked candidates to apply for permanent residence, in draws.
  4. If you are invited, you complete the application, upload your documents and pay the fees, then IRCC makes its decision.

The three programs

Federal Skilled Worker

At least 1 year (or 1,560 hours) of skilled work (TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3) in the same occupation within the last 10 years; approved language test results (CLB 7 minimum for the first official language); completed Canadian education or a foreign credential with an assessment (ECA); 67 points out of 100 on the selection factors; sufficient funds, unless exempt.

Federal Skilled Trades

At least 2 years of full-time work (or 3,120 hours) in a skilled trade within the last 5 years; a full-time job offer of at least 1 year or a Canadian certificate of qualification in the trade; approved language test.

Canadian Experience Class

At least 1 year (or 1,560 hours) of skilled work (TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3) in Canada within the last 3 years, with a temporary status that allowed you to work; approved language test. No education requirement.

In all cases, you must be admissible to Canada and plan to live outside Quebec (Quebec has its own selection).

What has changed since November 3, 2026

According to IRCC (announcement of October 16, 2026), the system now gives additional points for eligible Canadian work experience or an eligible job offer in a high-wage occupation (an occupation whose median wage is at least 1.3 times that of all Canadian workers). An LMIA is no longer needed for a job offer to give these points or to meet the job offer requirements of the Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades programs.

Scores of candidates already in the pool are recalculated automatically. IRCC states that the recalculation can take up to two weeks and that draws are paused during that time. To receive the job offer points, the profile had to be updated before November 3.

Eligible job offer

100 points if the occupation's median wage is at least 2 times the average median wage; 70 points between 1.5 and 2 times; 50 points between 1.3 and 1.5 times; 0 points for an occupation that is not high-wage.

Canadian experience in a high-wage occupation

In addition to the points for length of work, at least 12 months (1,560 hours) of eligible experience gives 70 points (at least 2 times), 40 points (at least 1.5 times) or 20 points (at least 1.3 times).

LMIA

No longer needed and no longer gives extra points or priority.

These rules evolve: the state described is that of November 5, 2026. Check your current situation with us.

Frequently asked questions

Which programs does Express Entry manage?

The Federal Skilled Worker Program, the Federal Skilled Trades Program and the Canadian Experience Class. Part of the Provincial Nominee Program is also linked to it.

Do I need to have worked in Canada?

Not for the Federal Skilled Worker Program, which can count experience gained abroad. It is however required for the Canadian Experience Class.

Is an LMIA always needed for a job offer?

According to IRCC, since November 3, 2026, an LMIA is no longer needed for a job offer to give the new points or meet the job offer requirements of the Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades programs.

What does “high-wage occupation” mean?

According to IRCC, it is an occupation whose median wage is at least 1.3 times that of all Canadian workers. IRCC publishes and updates the list.

Do you guarantee an invitation?

No. IRCC invites the top-ranked candidates in draws: nobody can guarantee an invitation or a result.
